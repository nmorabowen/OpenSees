### `PressureDependMultiYield03` hard-coded its critical-state line (`ei = 0.6, cs1 = 0.9, cs2 = 0.02, cs3 = 0.7`) — and its per-material reallocation overwrote every material's constants with the newest one's (WP-133)
- **Bites:** PDMY01/02 take `e, volLimit1..3` as arguments; PDMY03 fixed them in the constructor, so its void ratio always started at 0.6 whatever density the other parameters describe, and the critical-state line could not be retuned. Behind the hard-coding sat a latent defect: the constants live in static arrays indexed by `matN`, reallocated every 20 materials (`if (matCount%20 == 0)`), and the copy loop wrote `einitx[i] = ei; volLimit1x[i] = cs1; ...` for every EXISTING material `i` (the NEW material's values), leaking the old arrays. Harmless while all values were equal; a silent cross-material leak the moment they are user-set (material 21 would rewrite materials 1–20).
- **Workaround/status:** FIXED WP-133 (#866): optional flags `-ei -cs1 -cs2 -cs3` after all positional args, both parsers; the reallocation copies each slot's own values. Rule for any vanilla material that keeps per-material data in static `...x[matN]` arrays (the whole PDMY/PIMY family): a new per-material field needs THREE edits — the constructor store, the `matCount%20` copy loop, and the `recvSelf` reallocation — and a gate that creates >20 materials after the one under test (`tests/test_wp133_pdmy03_cs_params.py` G3). Note `matCount` and the arrays are process-wide and never reset on `wipe`, so the reallocation point depends on how many PDMY03 materials the PROCESS has created, not the model.
