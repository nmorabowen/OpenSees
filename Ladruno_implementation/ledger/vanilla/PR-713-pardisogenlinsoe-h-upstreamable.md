| `SRC/system_of_eqn/linearSOE/pardiso/PARDISOGenLinSOE.{h,cpp}` | `// Ladruno TIMs item 2` (ADR-75 P1d follow-up): **the `addA` half-storage asymmetry guard sampled STARTUP ONLY.** P1d's guard catches an unsymmetric element matrix whose lower half `-matrixType 1|2` is about to discard, but it was budgeted to the first `ASYM_CHECK_BUDGET`=3 tangent assemblies of the pattern (armed in `setSize`, spent one unit per pass in `zeroA`) — so the exact reported case, a material that is symmetric while elastic and only turns unsymmetric after first yield (non-associated flow, `rho_bar != rho`; ManzariDafalias measured at 4-6%), spent the whole budget elastic and was never checked again. Contact closing mid-run is the same hole. The in-source comment stated it as a flat LIMITATION rather than fixing it. **Fix:** a new `asymPass` member counts tangent assemblies of the current pattern (incremented in `zeroA`, re-anchored in `setSize`) and RE-ARMS the budget every `ASYM_RESAMPLE_PERIOD`=64 assemblies while `asymWarned` is unlatched — so the guard samples startup AND periodically, and late-onset asymmetry is reported within ≤64 assemblies of appearing. The once-per-SOE `asymWarned` latch is unchanged (it stops the re-arming for good), and the warning now names the assembly it fired on so the user can correlate it with the load step where the material yielded. **ADR-75's performance contract holds by arithmetic:** steady-state duty cycle is `ASYM_CHECK_BUDGET/ASYM_RESAMPLE_PERIOD` = 3/64 ≈ 4.7% of the mirror-compare cost, and that compare is the cheap half of `addA` (idSize²/2 in-cache subtractions of values already fetched, against an idSize² binary-searched scatter, no allocation, no extra traversal); 96.9% of assemblies pay one modulo in `zeroA` — once per assembly, not per element. Still a sampler, not a proof: an asymmetry that appears and vanishes entirely inside one 64-assembly sleep window is missable, and `-matrixType 2` still requires the author to know their tangent is symmetric. Gated by `tests/test_pardiso_asym_rearm.py`, which asserts not just that the warning fires but that the assembly number it reports is ≥64 — i.e. that it came from a RE-ARM, which the pre-change guard could not produce. | [#713](https://github.com/nmorabowen/OpenSees/pull/713) |
