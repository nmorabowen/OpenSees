# ADR-80 gates G1 / G2 / G3

> Completes **P0**. G4 is in [[80a_sp_gate_g4_auto_handler_2026-08-04]].
> Run on a **synthetic re-derivation**, which the ADR explicitly permits in
> place of the Cerro Lindo fuse decks ("P0 needs the Cerro Lindo fuse decks
> … or a synthetic re-derivation of the same geometry").
>
> Deck: `Ladruno_implementation/sp_gates/g123_predictor_gates.tcl`
> Artifact: `sp_gates/g123_predictor_gates_2026-08-04.json`

## The model

A 1D chain of `stdBrick`s: `L` = 100, cross-section 10×10, `ux`/`uy` fixed
everywhere and **ν = 0**, so `σ = E·ε` exactly and every number below is
hand-checkable. Base face `uz` fixed; top face `uz` driven by a non-homogeneous
`sp` (**disp**) or by the equivalent traction `F = E·A·δ/L` (**trac**).
`E` = 200 000, `LadrunoJ2` with `f_y` = 379.5 and `H_iso` = 2 000 (`H/E` = 1 %),
δ = 0.15 over 10 steps, `constraints Transformation`, `test NormDispIncr 1e-10`.

**The sizing is the whole point.** Converged strain is `δ/L` = 0.0015 ⇒
**σ = 300 MPa < f_y = 379.5**, so the converged answer *never yields*; but the
driven layer's per-step overstrain is `Δδ/h`, which at `h` = 5 gives a trial
σ of 600 MPa. That is the Cerro Lindo signature exactly (converged 295 MPa
against `f_y` 379.5, cover never yields, yet the run costs ×28.6).

**Verified empirically, not just asserted:** under the traction drive the J2 bar
reaches `u_top` = 0.150000 — bit-for-bit the elastic answer — so the material
demonstrably stays elastic. Every run in the matrix converges to the same
`u_top`. **The entire penalty is spurious.**

## G0 — the mechanism (drive × material), `N` = 20, 10 steps

| drive | elastic | J2 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **prescribed displacement** | 20 | **60** | **×3.00** |
| traction | 20 | 20 | ×1.00 |

**Reproduced.** Giving the driven layer a path-dependent law costs ×3.00 under a
prescribed displacement and **exactly nothing** under an equivalent traction —
the same qualitative split as Cerro Lindo's ×28.6 / ×0.95, on a model with no
cutbacks and 22 DOF-scale cost.

⚠ **The magnitude does not transfer and should not be quoted as if it did.**
Cerro Lindo's ×28.6 included **52 cutbacks** on an adaptive march; a fixed
10-step march cannot produce that amplification. This harness validates the
**mechanism and its scaling laws**, not the size of the penalty.

## G1 — does the penalty scale with `L/h`?

| `N` | `h` | `L/h` | elastic | J2 | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 20.0 | 5 | 20 | 28 | ×1.40 |
| 10 | 10.0 | 10 | 20 | 48 | ×2.40 |
| 20 | 5.0 | 20 | 20 | 60 | ×3.00 |
| 40 | 2.5 | 40 | 20 | 60 | ×3.00 |
| 80 | 1.25 | 80 | 20 | 60 | ×3.00 |

**Confirmed in direction — refining the driven layer makes it worse — but it
SATURATES.** The ADR predicted the penalty would "fall roughly linearly with
coarsening and grow with refinement". The monotone growth is real; the *linear*
part exists only in the transition band (`N` = 5 → 20), after which the ratio
plateaus at ×3.00 and further refinement buys nothing.

That plateau is mechanistically required, not an artifact: the driven layer
spuriously yields when `σ_conv(n) + E·Δδ/h > f_y`. With `Δδ` = 0.015,
`E·Δδ/h` = 3000/`h` — that is 150 MPa at `h` = 20 (yields only in late steps,
hence the mild ×1.40) and 600 MPa at `h` = 5 (yields from step 1). Once *every*
step spuriously yields and the consistent tangent has fully collapsed toward
`2G·H/(3G+H)`, there is nothing left to degrade. **The mechanism's sharpest
prediction survives, with "monotone and saturating" replacing "linear".**

## G2 — does the penalty scale with step size?

| steps | `Δδ` | elastic | J2 | **ratio** | J2 iters/step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.03000 | 10 | 30 | **×3.00** | 6.00 |
| 10 | 0.01500 | 20 | 60 | **×3.00** | 6.00 |
| 20 | 0.00750 | 40 | 96 | **×2.40** | 4.80 |
| 40 | 0.00375 | 80 | 116 | **×1.45** | 2.90 |

**Settled — and it dissolves the contradiction the ADR flagged.** The ADR
recorded a prior observation that cut against this gate: *"halving the step made
convergence worse, 19 → 27 iterations."* Both readings are correct and they
measure different things:

- **Total** iterations **rise** with smaller steps (30 → 116) — you are simply
  doing more steps.
- The **penalty ratio** against the elastic control **falls** (×3.00 → ×1.45) —
  per unit of progress, smaller steps genuinely reduce the pathology.

The prior observation counted the first quantity. The gate asked for the second
("measured per unit of **progress**, not per successful increment"). No
contradiction survives. Consistent with G1: at `Δδ` = 0.00375 the overstrain is
only 150 MPa, so only late steps spuriously yield.

## G3 — which half dominates, and what it actually bounds

`LadrunoJ2` **does** override `getInitialTangent()` with a genuine elastic
tangent (`LadrunoJ2.cpp:431`), so `Newton -initial` is a real substitution here
and **not** silently full Newton — the [[ladruno-adr76-tangent-reuse]] trap
(`NDMaterial::getInitialTangent()` defaulting to `getTangent()`) does not apply.
Checked before running, because it would have made this gate vacuous.

| algorithm | elastic | J2 | excess over elastic |
|---|---|---|---|
| `KrylovNewton` (consistent tangent) | 20 | 60 | **40** |
| `Newton -initial` (elastic tangent) | 20 | 37 | **17** |

Substituting the elastic tangent removes the collapsed-tangent half while
leaving the spurious residual intact:

- **collapsed tangent: 23/40 = 58 %**
- **overstrained residual: 17/40 = 42 %**

**Both halves are real and comparable, with the tangent half slightly larger.**
Abaqus doctrine predicted the *residual* half would dominate; measured, it is the
smaller one. The margin is modest and this is one synthetic model — worth
recording, not worth generalising.

### ⚠ The ADR's inference from G3 is wrong, and this is the important part

ADR-80 §"Validation gates" states: *"This also bounds how much a predictor can
buy, since a predictor attacks the residual half directly."*

**That does not follow, and P2 should not be scoped as if it did.** The two
halves are not independent contributions to be split between remedies — the
tangent half is *downstream of the same overstrain*. The consistent tangent
collapses **because** the layer spuriously yields, and it spuriously yields
**because** it was overstrained. A predictor that removes the overstrain removes
the spurious yielding, and therefore removes the collapsed tangent along with the
bad residual.

So G3 measures **how the cost decomposes given that the overstrain happens**. It
does **not** bound the predictor at 42 %. The predictor's ceiling on this model
is the full 40-iteration excess — i.e. the ×3.00 collapsing toward ×1.00 — not
some 42 % fraction of it. **Correct the ADR before writing P2's acceptance
criteria**, or S1 will be graded against a target that is wrong by more than a
factor of two.

**Scope limit on that claim — self-imposed, and it matters.** "The predictor's
ceiling is the full excess" holds **only where the yielding is entirely
spurious**, which is how this model is deliberately built (converged σ = 300 MPa
against `f_y` = 379.5; nothing yields physically, confirmed by the traction run
landing on the exact elastic answer). **In a model that genuinely yields, part of
the tangent collapse is real** — the material *is* plastic at the converged state
— and no predictor recovers that part. There the ceiling is the excess
attributable to the spurious fraction alone, which **this gate has not
measured**. Do not carry the "full excess" statement over to a plastically
hinging model. Cerro Lindo sits on the safe side of the line (its cover never
yields), so S1's field target is unaffected — but the general statement is
narrower than the measurement, and saying so is cheaper than being caught by it.

## Verdict

**P0 complete. S1 is GO on mechanism grounds.**

- The mechanism reproduces on a clean synthetic model with an exactly known
  answer, and reproduces the drive-asymmetry that is its signature (G0).
- Its sharpest prediction — penalty scales with `L/h` — survives, refined to
  "monotone, then saturating" (G1).
- The step-size question is settled and the recorded counter-observation is
  explained away rather than ignored (G2).
- Both halves of the pathology are live, and — contra the ADR's own reasoning —
  a predictor addresses both (G3).

# G5 — adaptive march with cutbacks (added same day, closes a blind spot in the above)

## Why this was needed

Everything above marches a **fixed** number of steps that always succeed, so it
can only measure *iterations within successful steps*. But the Cerro Lindo ×28.6
was dominated by **52 cutbacks** — a failure mode a fixed march cannot exhibit at
all. Proposing that harness as S1's *primary* acceptance gate was therefore a
mistake: **it was structurally blind to the very thing that made the field case
expensive.** `sp_gates/g5_adaptive_cutbacks.tcl` closes that gap. Same physics
and sizing; the only change is a caller-driven adaptive ladder (halve on failure,
double after two successes) that counts cutbacks.

## The iteration budget is the control parameter, so sweep it — don't pick one

Cutbacks are not intrinsic to the model. They appear only when `maxIter` falls
**between** the clean per-step cost (2 iterations) and the penalized one (6).
Reporting a single hand-picked budget would be choosing the answer.

| `maxIter` | **disp + J2** (inc/cutbacks/iters) | disp + elastic | trac + J2 | iters vs elastic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **43 / 23 / 178** | 6 / 0 / 12 | 6 / 0 / 12 | **×14.8** |
| 4 | **43 / 23 / 201** | 6 / 0 / 12 | 6 / 0 / 12 | **×16.8** |
| 5 | **43 / 23 / 224** | 6 / 0 / 12 | 6 / 0 / 12 | **×18.7** |
| 6 | 6 / 0 / 36 | 6 / 0 / 12 | 6 / 0 / 12 | ×3.0 |
| 8 | 6 / 0 / 36 | 6 / 0 / 12 | 6 / 0 / 12 | ×3.0 |
| 12 | 6 / 0 / 36 | 6 / 0 / 12 | 6 / 0 / 12 | ×3.0 |

**The cliff is sharp and it sits exactly at the penalized per-step cost.** At
`maxIter` ≥ 6 the prescribed-displacement J2 run completes in 6 increments with
no cutbacks; at `maxIter` ≤ 5 it explodes to **43 increments and 23 cutbacks**.

**Both controls are completely insensitive to the budget** — elastic-under-disp
and J2-under-traction sit at 6/0/12 in *every* row. So the explosion is specific
to *prescribed displacement × path-dependent material*, which is the claim.
Every run still lands on `u_top` = 0.15 exactly; the answer never moves.

## What this changes

| | increments | cutbacks | iterations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Field** (Cerro Lindo) | ×10.8 | 52 | **×28.6** |
| **Synthetic G5** (`maxIter` = 5) | ×7.2 | 23 | **×18.7** |
| ~~Synthetic, fixed march (G0–G3)~~ | ×1.0 | *impossible* | ×3.0 |

The fixed march understated the penalty by roughly a factor of six, entirely
because it could not cut back. With cutbacks enabled the synthetic model lands
in the same regime as the field case on all three reported quantities. **The
earlier "the magnitude does not transfer" caveat was really "the magnitude does
not transfer *through a fixed march*"** — the mechanism does reproduce at field
scale once the march can fail.

## Corrected S1 acceptance gate (supersedes the one written above)

Use **G5 at `maxIter` = 5**, not the fixed march:

- `-extrapolate 0` reproduces **43 / 23 / 224** exactly (bit-identical-to-stock).
- `-extrapolate 1.0` drives it toward the elastic control **6 / 0 / 12**.
  **Cutbacks going to zero is the headline number** — it is the quantity the
  field case is actually made of, and the one a fixed march cannot report.
- Keep the fixed harness as a secondary, faster smoke gate (60 → 20).
- Both controls must stay pinned at 6 / 0 / 12, and `u_top` must stay 0.15.

⚠ A predictor that improved iterations-per-step while leaving the cutback count
unchanged would pass the old gate and be nearly worthless in the field. That is
precisely the failure this gate now catches.
