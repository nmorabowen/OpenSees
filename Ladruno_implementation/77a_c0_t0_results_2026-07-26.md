# ADR-77 C0 + T0 — first measurements

> Companion results doc to [[77_ladruno_implicit_transient_adr]]. C0 is the
> consistency audit (source, no timing); T0 is the five-loop step anatomy.
> Bench: this PC, serial, `MKL_NUM_THREADS=1`, LadrunoBrick + LadrunoJ2 cube,
> Newmark(0.5,0.25) + Newton + `NormDispIncr 1e-7`, dt=2e-4.
> Harness `Ladruno_files/testbed/perf/phase77/{t0_step_anatomy.py, t0_report.py}`.

## 1. Headline — the ADR's own §3 hypothesis is NOT confirmed

ADR-77 §3 reasoned: *"with PARDISO the element loop is ~75% of a solve-bound lane
(L3-0). If the transient step behaves the same way, the target is the assembly
side."* **Gate G0 forbade assuming that transplant, and G0 earned its keep.**

Measured at n=15 (11 520 DOF, 3 375 elements, 4 steps):

| | UmfPack | PARDISO | L3-0 static lane (for contrast) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `elem.tangent` | 30.4% | **40.8%** | 35.8% → **74.9%** |
| `linearSolve` | 58.5% | **45.2%** | (minor after flip) |

**On the transient step, `linearSolve` remains the single largest loop even under
PARDISO (45.2% > 40.8%).** The element loop rises — the direction matches L3-0 —
but nowhere near the 74.9% that would have made assembly the obvious target. Wall:
6.399 s → 4.662 s, **1.37x**, answers bit-identical (`uz` relerr 0.0e+00).

**Caveat, stated up front and not resolved by this run:** these are **1-thread**
numbers, chosen so the run is a bit-exact oracle (threaded PARDISO is not
byte-reproducible, ADR-75 P1f). PARDISO's advantage grows with threads, which
would push `linearSolve` down and the element share up. L3-0's 74.9% was not
measured under the same thread count. **A thread sweep is the first T0 follow-up
(T0b) and no assembly-side conclusion should be drawn before it runs.**

Sanity check that the measurement is sound: the assembly loops are
solver-independent, as they must be — `formTangent` 1999.8 ms (Umf) vs 1985.8 ms
(PARDISO), `elem.residual` 335.50 ms in *both*. Only `linearSolve` moves. Profiler
overhead is +1.9% (Umf) / +3.3% (PARDISO) on step wall (profiled run is separate
from the timed samples, never mixed in).

## 3. G2 verdict — the constant-M/C cache is NOT authorized

**G2 headroom (element inertia re-formed every iteration + both DOF_Group loops):**

| | UmfPack | PARDISO |
|---|---:|---:|
| headroom | 211.3 ms | 210.8 ms |
| % of step | **3.24%** | **4.38%** |
| G2 gate | ≥ 10% | ≥ 10% |

**Fails the gate by better than 2x on both arms ⇒ the constant-M/C cache dies here,
at zero design cost.** This is exactly what T2 was specified to do: kill a
plausible-sounding lever before anyone designs it. ADR-77 §4.3 item 4 is closed
unless the caveat below reopens it.

**The one caveat that could reopen G2:** this deck carries its mass in the
*material* (`LadrunoJ2 -rho`), so `dof.tangent` measures almost nothing (0.27%).
A model with lumped nodal mass (`mass` commands — i.e. most building models) puts
real work in the DOF_Group loop. **Re-run G2 on a nodal-mass deck before closing
it for good.** That is now a T2 subtask, not a conclusion.

## 4. What the new instrumentation cost and bought

Two vanilla files, one coarse scope each (`// Ladruno ADR-77 T0`):

- `SRC/analysis/integrator/TransientIntegrator.cpp` — `dof.tangent`
- `SRC/analysis/integrator/IncrementalIntegrator.cpp` — `dof.residual`

Both loops were untimed; their cost was silently folded into "the rest of
`formTangent`/`formUnbalance`". The measurement confirms the P1f structural claim
that motivated ADR-77: **`IncrementalIntegrator::formTangent` (the static path)
has no DOF_Group loop at all — only the transient path assembles nodal mass.**
That asymmetry is now visible in the profile rather than only in the source.

## 5. C0 — consistency audit findings

Method note: the first pass was `grep`-based and produced **two false positives**
(Newmark reported as missing a `deltaT` guard — it has a *more* careful one:
`deltaT < 0.0 || (displ != 3 && deltaT == 0.0)`; and `StagedNewmark` reported as
having a stub `sendSelf` — it has *none*, inheriting Newmark's working one, and
declares no members of its own). **Every cell below was confirmed by reading the
function.** C0 is a read-the-source audit; grep only nominates candidates.

| # | Finding | Classes | Severity | G5 verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C0-1** | `newStep(deltaT)` takes no validity guard and divides: `c2 = 2.0/deltaT`, `c3 = 4.0/(deltaT*deltaT)`. `analyze(n, 0.0)` yields inf/nan in the tangent instead of a clean error return. Newmark/HHT/HHTGeneralized/GeneralizedAlpha/Collocation/WilsonTheta/Newmark1 all guard. | `TRBDF2`, `TRBDF3`, `BackwardEuler`, `Houbolt` | garbage-run, no diagnostic | **patch-in-place** — adding a guard cannot change results on valid input |
| **C0-2** | `formEleTangent` has no `HALL_TANGENT` branch and **fails silently**: `BackwardEuler`/`Newmark1` `zeroTangent()` then add nothing ⇒ **a zero tangent**; `Collocation` adds `c2·C + c3·M` with **no stiffness at all**. `Newmark` at least prints `unknown FLAG`. `algorithm Newton -hall` reaches any integrator. | `BackwardEuler`, `Newmark1`, `Collocation` | **silent wrong answer** — the worst class | **patch-in-place** — add the branch, or at minimum the error return Newmark has |
| **C0-3** | `sendSelf`/`recvSelf` are stubs returning 0 without sending. Benign *today* (all three are parameterless), but latent: any future ctor argument is silently dropped in parallel/database use. Same shape as the contact handler's P1a stubs. | `TRBDF2`, `TRBDF3`, `Houbolt` | latent | **patch-in-place**, low priority |
| **C0-4** | `static bool converged` at **file scope** in `Newmark.cpp:56` — written in `newStep` (true) and `revertToLastStep` (false), and its **only read is inside a commented-out debug block**. Dead mutable global shared by every Newmark instance; the static-idiom hazard ADR-75b §5.4 catalogued, for zero benefit. | `Newmark` | dead state / re-entrancy hazard | **patch-in-place** — delete (removing dead state cannot change results) |

**No C0 finding requires owning the class.** All four are small, greppable,
result-preserving vanilla diffs — the §4.2 pattern, and evidence *for* the ADR's
Q1b position rather than against it. They batch into one consistency-wave PR with
a `LEDGER_vanilla_files.md` row each, and are candidates for the upstream-PR
campaign.

C0-2 is the one worth prioritising: a silent zero tangent is worse than an
unsupported-option error, because the run *completes* and reports numbers.

## 6. Gotchas banked

- **The fork's parallel numberer does not exist in a serial build.** The
  registered verbs are `LadrunoParallelRCM` / `LadrunoParallelPlain` (ADR-30
  §140's `numberer LadrunoParallel` is doc shorthand and is rejected outright),
  and **both factories are wrapped in `#ifdef _PARALLEL_INTERPRETERS`** —
  `OpenSeesCommands.cpp:4774` returns 0 in serial. A null numberer makes OpenSees
  fall back to **RCM with only a warning**, so a serial bench that asks for the
  Ladruno numberer silently measures RCM. Same family as the P1d "a mistyped
  `system` costs you the SOLVER, silently" trap. **Serial T0 runs RCM by design;
  the Ladruno numberer is a T4 (parallel) knob.**
- `h5py` is **not** in the `python3.12` used to drive the bench; it is in
  `C:\Users\nmb\venv\opensees_env\Scripts\python.exe` (3.15.1). Drive the bench
  with `python3.12 -S`, read the profiles with the venv interpreter.
- The profiled run must never be one of the timed samples (it costs +2-3%).
  `t0_step_anatomy.py` runs it separately for this reason.

## 6b. T0b — the thread sweep, and the answer to §1's open question

**T0's 1-thread result WAS a 1-thread artifact. The assembly-side premise is
restored — but only under threaded PARDISO, and the crossover is at 2 threads.**

n=15, 4 steps, 2 repeats, ladder 1/2/4/8/16 (i7-13700KF, 8 P + 8 E cores). Each
thread count is a separate process: MKL reads `MKL_NUM_THREADS` at library init,
so an in-process loop would silently measure the first setting for every arm.

### PARDISO

| thr | wall_s | step_ms | `elem.tangent` | (ms) | `linearSolve` | (ms) | assembly total | verdict |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 4.780 | 4872 | 40.6% | 1976 | **45.6%** | 2223 | 47.8% | solve > elem |
| 2 | 3.811 | 3935 | **50.6%** | 1989 | 32.2% | 1267 | 59.6% | **elem > solve** |
| 4 | **3.519** | 3652 | **54.9%** | 2006 | 26.3% | 960 | **64.7%** | elem > solve |
| 8 | 3.563 | 3720 | 59.2% | 2202 | 20.7% | 771 | 69.9% | elem > solve |
| 16 | 3.921 | 4058 | 62.7% | 2543 | 16.5% | 671 | **73.9%** | elem > solve |

### UmfPack (control — serial factorization)

| thr | wall_s | `elem.tangent` | `linearSolve` | verdict |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 6.459 | 30.1% | 58.8% | solve > elem |
| 4 | 5.609 | 35.0% | 52.1% | solve > elem |
| 16 | 6.383 | 38.2% | 48.3% | solve > elem |

**UmfPack never crosses over at any thread count.** So the flip is a property of
*PARDISO*, not of the transient path — the right statement is "a fast threaded
solver makes assembly dominant", not "transient steps are assembly-bound". At 16
threads PARDISO's assembly total reaches **73.9%**, essentially L3-0's 74.9%.

### The measurement is trustworthy in the ≤4 rows only

`elem.tangent` **absolute ms** is the control variable, and it is flat to 4
threads (**1.5%** spread PARDISO, **0.3%** UmfPack — the element kernels work on
24x24 matrices, so MKL runs them serially, as expected). It then **rises** at
8/16 threads (1976 → 2543 ms), **identically in both arms**, which rules out any
solver-specific cause: those are MKL worker threads (spin-wait by default)
contending with the serial element loop on a hybrid P/E part. Consequence:

- the ≤4-thread rows are clean and carry the conclusion;
- the 8/16 rows must **not** be read as "assembly got worse" — the share is real
  but the ms is contaminated;
- **more threads is worse past 4 on this deck**: PARDISO wall bottoms at 3.519 s
  (4 thr) and degrades to 3.921 s (16 thr). ADR-75's 4-thread default is
  vindicated on the transient path too.

### Two side-confirmations

- **Threaded PARDISO non-reproducibility (P1f) reproduced independently.**
  `uz` relerr is exactly 0.0e+00 at 1 and 2 threads, and 1.1e-16 at 4/8/16. The
  ADR-75b policy (1 thread for oracle runs) is correct and now has a second
  witness on a different deck and analysis type.
- **G2 stays dead at every thread count.** Headroom rises only because the step
  shrinks: 4.43% (1 thr) → 5.87% (4) → 6.85% (16), never approaching the 10%
  gate. The constant-M/C cache is closed on this deck regardless of threading;
  only the nodal-mass re-run (§3) could reopen it.

### What T0b means for the cross-ADR picture

`elem.tangent` alone is **50-63% of the step at 2-16 threads** on the implicit
transient path under PARDISO. ADR-75b's L3-0b de-authorized threaded assembly
(L3-1) after `-commitSolveState` dropped its loop-A share to 38.95% on an
**explicit** deck. Those are different loops in different taxonomies and this
result does **not** by itself re-authorize L3-1 — but it does say the *implicit
transient* path is a materially better threading candidate than the explicit path
that de-authorized it, and ADR-75b should re-ask the question with its own loop
taxonomy on this lane. Recorded as a hand-off, not a decision.

## 7. What this changes in ADR-77

1. **§3's assembly-side premise is RESTORED, with a precise condition attached**
   (T0b): it holds under threaded PARDISO from **2 threads up** (assembly 59.6%
   at 2 thr, 64.7% at 4 thr) and fails at 1 thread (47.8%) and under UmfPack at
   every thread count. The correct claim is *"a fast threaded solver makes
   assembly dominant"*, not *"transient steps are assembly-bound"*. G0 was right
   to demand the measurement: the answer is **thread-** and **solver-dependent**,
   which neither the L3-0 transplant nor T0 alone would have revealed.
2. **G2 (constant-M/C cache) fails its gate at every thread count** (4.4% → 6.9%)
   ⇒ not authorized; one nodal-mass re-run outstanding before permanent closure.
3. **C0 delivers 4 confirmed patch-in-place findings, 0 requiring ownership** —
   consistent with the ADR's Q1b position, now with evidence.
4. Bench-stack correction: serial T0 is **RCM**, not the Ladruno numberer; and
   **`MKL_NUM_THREADS=4` is the operating point** for this deck (wall degrades
   past 4 on a hybrid P/E part).
5. **T1 is now the live stage**, and T0b sharpens its question: with assembly at
   ~65% of a 4-thread step, the reuse levers worth measuring are the ones that
   skip *assembly* (ModifiedNewton, `-factoronce`), not the ones that skip
   factorization. The textbook ranking may well invert, exactly as L3-0b warned.
